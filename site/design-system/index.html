    Clepso · Brand and design system · v1.0

    The limit, and the live signal.

    01 / LOGO

    Logo

    A heartbeat inside a ring. The ring is the limit, the same ring that sits in the menu bar; the beat is the live reading. One idea that reads at 16 pixels, in one colour, in any theme.

    Ring · the limitBeat · the live reading

    Clepso watches something you cannot see, a usage limit, and tells you before you hit it. The mark puts the two things that matter one inside the other: the container that fills and the signal that keeps arriving.

    Because the ring is the existing menu bar ring, the logo and the status icon are one object. The beat is the live reading; the same ring holds a warning, a flatline, a clock, a broken ring for demo data, dots while it waits for a first reading and nothing at all when not connected, without the brand changing shape.

    It is drawn on a 16-pixel grid with a 1-pixel stroke, the way the original Macintosh icons were drawn: judged at actual size first, large second.

    How it came about

    Six directions on the same grid, judged at menu bar size. The two small renders beside each are actual size (16 px) and 2×.

    1. A ECG lineRejected

      The favicon's heartbeat, alone. Says 'health app'; every fitness tracker owns it. Without a container it has no silhouette at 16 px and cannot carry a status.

    2. B Lit coreRejected

      The 1.11 menu bar ring with a 2 × 2 core. Clean, but reads as a radio button or a record light: a control, not a product.

    3. C P monogramRejected

      Ownable as a letter, empty as an idea, and tied to a working name that may change. A pixel P also blurs into 'D' and 'R' at 16 px.

    4. D GaugeRejected

      Speedometer cliché. The needle's angle is illegible at 16 px, and it promises a precise reading the app often cannot give (stale and missing are first-class states).

    5. E Signal barsRejected

      Reads as Wi-Fi, audio level or cell signal. Strong silhouette, wrong meaning.

    6. F Beat in the ringChosen

      Chosen. The ring is the limit (and the existing menu bar ring); the beat is the live reading. One idea, legible at 16 px, and it can flatline, warn or go stale without changing shape.

    Refining the beat

    Four drawings of the chosen idea. The last floats with a 2 p margin and is centred on both axes.

    1. F1 Trace edge to edge

      The line splits the ring into halves: reads as a theta or a null sign.

    2. F2 Heavy beat

      Two-pixel peak clots at 16 px and fights the 1 p ring.

    3. F3 Square screen

      An oscilloscope, but also a generic chart icon; loses the menu bar ring.

    4. F4 Floating trace

      Chosen: the beat floats with a 2 p margin, centred on both axes.

    Lockups

    The horizontal lockup is primary. Use the stacked lockup where width is scarce, and the symbol alone where the name is already present: the menu bar, the app icon, a favicon.

    Construction

    Unit p is 1/16 of the symbol. Every edge falls on whole p; the stroke is 1 p everywhere. Wordmark capitals are 7 p, tracked 2 p; the horizontal gap is 5 p; clear space is 4 p on every side.

    Clear space

    4 p on every side: a quarter of the symbol's height. Nothing else enters it, including the edge of the screen or page.

    Minimum size

    Symbol 16 px (1 p = 1 px). Horizontal lockup 54 × 16 px. In print, symbol 6 mm, lockup 20 mm wide.

    Scaling

    Below 64 px, scale only in whole multiples (16, 32, 48, 64 px) so no pixel is resampled. Above 64 px, any size.

    Small symbol

    A 12 p drawing of the same mark, for the 16 px app icon and the favicon only, where 16 p cannot sit 1:1.

    Colour

    Always one flat colour. On dark grounds the signature is phosphor green; on paper, deep green; anywhere else, black or white.

    Across themes

    In the app the mark takes the active theme's accent. The shape never changes, so recognition never depends on green.

    Misuse

    The mark is pixels on a grid, in one colour, with room around it. Each example below breaks one of those.

    • ✕Don’t stretch or squash it

    • ✕Don’t rotate it

    • ✕Don’t fill it with a gradient

    • ✕Don’t outline it

    • ✕Don’t add glows, shadows or bevels

    • ✕Don’t resample it off the grid

    Menu bar

    A template image: opaque pixels on transparency, tinted by macOS for light, dark and tinted menu bars. The beat is the brand; the status family keeps the ring and changes what is inside, so no state rests on colour. When none of the readings behind the shape is live, the ring opens at five o'clock with a dot in the gap. The menu bar rotates Claude Code and Codex’s 5-hour and 7-day windows every six seconds, showing the agent, window and measured used percentage with that window’s state glyph. An old reading says OLD; a passed reset says RESET? until fresh evidence arrives. Settings lets you pin a window, retain the aggregate summary, or show the icon alone. Rotation pauses on hover, with Reduce Motion and with VoiceOver. In the aggregate summary, the flatline has no percentage and only live readings supply a percentage.

    03 / TYPOGRAPHY

    Typography

    Two faces drawn for Clepso, one geometry. Clepso Pixel speaks in numbers and labels; Clepso Mono explains. Both are built in code from the same grid logic, so the display face and the body face share corners, rhythm and figures.

    Pairing

    Pixel for numbers and short labels, usually uppercase. Mono for anything that is a sentence. Never a sentence in Clepso Pixel; never a big figure in Clepso Mono.

    Pixel snapping

    Clepso Pixel's pixel is 1/10 em. Use 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 pt in the app and 10, 20, 30, 50, 60, 80 px on the web; anything else blurs.

    No faux styles

    No synthetic bold or italic of the pixel face, no tracking on it (its spacing is in whole pixels already), no outlines or shadows on type.

    Fallback

    Clepso Mono covers ASCII and Latin-1. Keep the system monospace behind it: names outside the set fall back per character.

    05 / COMPONENTS

    Components

    The SwiftUI components behind the app, rendered from the Swift code by clepso-design-render. Every image here is the real component or the running app, in demo data.

    A number always says where it came fromEvery value shows its source and age. Components take provenance-carrying values; there is no bare-number input.
    Missing is never zeroAbsent, null, stale and not connected are four states with their own words. None renders as 0%.
    Never colour aloneEach state has a symbol, words, a bar pattern and a spoken phrase.
    Restyle without changing meaningPalette, spacing and layout can change. State names, labels and accessibility text are locked and tested.

    Screens

    The running app in demo data, captured from the signed bundle.

    Phosphor components

    The pixel layer. Every shape is drawn in code on a pixel grid, so it stays sharp and scales with text size.

    PixelLimitRow

    One allowance pool in the pixel style. Same status words, freshness, reset line and VoiceOver sentence as QuotaProgress (tested); the optional pace line always says “Estimate”.

    PixelBar

    The segmented bar. The fill follows the frame's stepped pixels: the lit and empty runs step one pixel in from the frame, so a one-pixel gap runs all the way round, corners included. Stale values are dithered and missing values draw only an outline, so meaning never rests on colour.

    PixelColumns

    Every bar chart (Home's last 30 days, when you work, tokens per day) is drawn here: whole-pixel widths, heights in whole pixels from the baseline, top corners stepped like the frame's, and a faint one-pixel stub for none.

    PixelButtonStyle

    Bevelled pixel buttons: a lit top edge and a shaded bottom edge that swap when pressed.

    PixelTabs

    Tabs in the pixel-pack manner, used in the Details window and Stats.

    PixelStat

    A value over a small pixel caption, for the session strip and Stats.

    PixelLineChart

    History drawn as steps snapped to a pixel grid, with dotted 80/95/100 guides, NOW, and the dashed pace projection. VoiceOver reads a sentence instead of the drawing.

    PixelHeatmap

    Activity like Claude Code's /stats: one column per week, Monday on top, four levels plus empty. Each day is a PixelLevelTile, so the level is pixel density, not shade.

    PixelLevelTile

    One day of an activity calendar on the 2 pt pixel grid, with notched corners. Levels are pixel density: a few lit pixels, a checkerboard, solid with holes, solid; the pattern says the level without relying on colour. No data is the outline only.     Whether readings are arriving, with the age of the last one. Never “100% connected”.

    DataSourceLabel

    Source and freshness, on every value. Freshness is never omitted; stale values say “last seen”.

    06 / VOICE AND TONE

    Voice and tone

    One voice: a precise instrument that tells the truth. The tone shifts with the moment; the voice never does.

    ExactNumbers carry their source and age. Units always. No rounding that changes a decision.
    PlainShort words, active verbs, sentence case. The product's job is to be glanced at.
    Honest about uncertaintyAn estimate says so; a missing value says so; a guess is never shown as a fact.
    CalmNo alarms for their own sake. Attention when a limit is near, plain facts when it is reached.

    Tone by moment

    Available

    Reported limits available

    Quiet. One line, then out of the way.

    Approaching

    Approaching limit · resets 20:01

    Direct. Lead with what will happen and when.

    Reached

    Limit reached · resets in 2h 14m

    Factual. What it is, when it ends. No blame, no exclamation marks.

    Missing

    Not reported yet

    Neutral. Name the absence; never fill it.
