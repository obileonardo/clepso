    01 / LOGO

    Logo

    A heartbeat inside a ring. The ring is the limit, the same ring that sits in the menu bar; the beat is the live reading. One idea that reads at 16 pixels, in one colour, in any theme.

    Ring · the limitBeat · the live reading

    Pulse watches something you cannot see, a usage limit, and tells you before you hit it. The mark puts the two things that matter one inside the other: the container that fills and the signal that keeps arriving.

    Because the ring is the existing menu bar ring, the logo and the status icon are one object. The beat is the "available" state; the same ring can hold a warning, a flatline or a clock without the brand changing shape.

    It is drawn on a 16-pixel grid with a 1-pixel stroke, the way the original Macintosh icons were drawn: judged at actual size first, large second.

    How it came about

    Six directions on the same grid, judged at menu bar size. The two small renders beside each are actual size (16 px) and 2×.

    1. A ECG lineRejected

      The favicon's heartbeat, alone. Says 'health app'; every fitness tracker owns it. Without a container it has no silhouette at 16 px and cannot carry a status.

    2. B Lit coreRejected

      The 1.11 menu bar ring with a 2 × 2 core. Clean, but reads as a radio button or a record light: a control, not a product.

    3. C P monogramRejected

      Ownable as a letter, empty as an idea, and tied to a working name that may change. A pixel P also blurs into 'D' and 'R' at 16 px.

    4. D GaugeRejected

      Speedometer cliché. The needle's angle is illegible at 16 px, and it promises a precise reading the app often cannot give (stale and missing are first-class states).

    5. E Signal barsRejected

      Reads as Wi-Fi, audio level or cell signal. Strong silhouette, wrong meaning.

    6. F Beat in the ringChosen

      Chosen. The ring is the limit (and the existing menu bar ring); the beat is the live reading. One idea, legible at 16 px, and it can flatline, warn or go stale without changing shape.

    Refining the beat

    Four drawings of the chosen idea. The last floats with a 2 p margin and is centred on both axes.

    1. F1 Trace edge to edge

      The line splits the ring into halves: reads as a theta or a null sign.

    2. F2 Heavy beat

      Two-pixel peak clots at 16 px and fights the 1 p ring.

    3. F3 Square screen

      An oscilloscope, but also a generic chart icon; loses the menu bar ring.

    4. F4 Floating trace

      Chosen: the beat floats with a 2 p margin, centred on both axes.

    Lockups

    The horizontal lockup is primary. Use the stacked lockup where width is scarce, and the symbol alone where the name is already present: the menu bar, the app icon, a favicon.

    Construction

    Unit p is 1/16 of the symbol. Every edge falls on whole p; the stroke is 1 p everywhere. Wordmark capitals are 7 p, tracked 2 p; the horizontal gap is 5 p; clear space is 4 p on every side.

    Clear space

    4 p on every side: a quarter of the symbol's height. Nothing else enters it, including the edge of the screen or page.

    Minimum size

    Symbol 16 px (1 p = 1 px). Horizontal lockup 54 × 16 px. In print, symbol 6 mm, lockup 20 mm wide.

    Scaling

    Below 64 px, scale only in whole multiples (16, 32, 48, 64 px) so no pixel is resampled. Above 64 px, any size.

    Small symbol

    A 12 p drawing of the same mark, for the 16 px app icon and the favicon only, where 16 p cannot sit 1:1.

    Colour

    Always one flat colour. On dark grounds the signature is phosphor green; on paper, deep green; anywhere else, black or white.

    Across themes

    In the app the mark takes the active theme's accent. The shape never changes, so recognition never depends on green.

    Misuse

    The mark is pixels on a grid, in one colour, with room around it. Each example below breaks one of those.

    • ✕Don’t stretch or squash it

    • ✕Don’t rotate it

    • ✕Don’t fill it with a gradient

    • ✕Don’t outline it

    • ✕Don’t add glows, shadows or bevels

    • ✕Don’t resample it off the grid

    • Pulse

      ✕Don’t retype the wordmark

    • ✕Don’t rearrange the lockup

    Never colour alone

    Every state has a symbol, words and a bar pattern as well as a colour (tested in the app). Recolouring can never merge two meanings.

    Accent is for signal

    Use the accent for the live figure, the mark and links, not for decoration or large fills.

    Status colours are reserved

    Attention and blocked colours appear only when a limit is near or reached.

    Pairing

    Pixel for numbers and short labels, usually uppercase. Mono for anything that is a sentence. Never a sentence in Pulse Pixel; never a big figure in Pulse Mono.

    Pixel snapping

    Pulse Pixel's pixel is 1/10 em. Use 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 pt in the app and 10, 20, 30, 50, 60, 80 px on the web; anything else blurs.

    No faux styles

    No synthetic bold or italic of the pixel face, no tracking on it (its spacing is in whole pixels already), no outlines or shadows on type.

    Fallback

    Pulse Mono covers ASCII and Latin-1. Keep the system monospace behind it: names outside the set fall back per character.

    Grid

    12 × 12 p, 1 p stroke, no anti-aliasing. Sizes are multiples of 6 pt (12, 18, 24) so pixels stay whole.

    Colour

    An icon takes the colour of the text beside it. Status colour only when the icon is the status.

    Shape carries meaning

    Within a family no two states share a shape. A new icon is drawn here first, at 1×, before it ships.

    Never mix

    No smooth system symbols beside pixel icons, and no icon redrawn at a finer grid to make it fit.

    Classic components (29 states)

    The first six components in the system theme that preceded Phosphor. Still in the code; the pixel components reuse their wording and accessibility text. Light or dark follows the theme you preview.

    ProviderCard

    One provider: identity, plan, one row per allowance pool, the active session. Compact in the menu bar popover, expanded in provider detail.

    QuotaProgress

    One allowance pool. Takes a provenance-carrying value, never a bare number, so missing data cannot render as 0%. Every state has its own symbol, words and bar pattern as well as colour.

    ResetCountdown

    Counts down from a verified reset instant. At the reset time it says “Reset expected”, never “Ready”: only a fresh reading can say that.

    PlanPill

    The plan label always carries how it is known. The plan never feeds any arithmetic.

    ConnectionHealth

    Whether readings are arriving, with the age of the last one. Never “100% connected”.

    DataSourceLabel

    Source and freshness, on every value. Freshness is never omitted; stale values say “last seen”.

    06 / VOICE AND TONE

    Voice and tone

    One voice: a precise instrument that tells the truth. The tone shifts with the moment; the voice never does.

    ExactNumbers carry their source and age. Units always. No rounding that changes a decision.
    PlainShort words, active verbs, sentence case. The product's job is to be glanced at.
    Honest about uncertaintyAn estimate says so; a missing value says so; a guess is never shown as a fact.
    CalmNo alarms for their own sake. Attention when a limit is near, plain facts when it is reached.

    Tone by moment

    Available

    Reported limits available

    Quiet. One line, then out of the way.

    Approaching

    Approaching limit · resets 20:01

    Direct. Lead with what will happen and when.

    Reached

    Limit reached · resets in 2h 14m

    Factual. What it is, when it ends. No blame, no exclamation marks.

    Missing

    Not reported yet

    Neutral. Name the absence; never fill it.
