What the tokens are

A token is a small piece of text, roughly three or four characters, that models read and write. Claude Code counts four kinds.

KindWhat it isTypical shareCounts for level
OutputText the model wrote: replies, code, tool calls. The model's own work.Well under 1%Yes
InputNew text sent to the model that was not already cached: your message, tool results, files it read.Under 1%No
Cache readEarlier conversation the model re-reads from the prompt cache on every turn. Grows with the length of a conversation, not with the work done.Most tokens, often 95% or moreNo
Cache writeConversation stored in the prompt cache so later turns can re-read it cheaply.A few percentNo

Totals (“15.8B tokens”, “79% of all”) add all four kinds, as /stats does. Levels use output only.

Levels

Level = 1 + the number of targets the model's all-time output has reached.
Targets: 1K, 2K, 5K, 10K, 20K, 50K, 100K … (1, 2, 5 in every power of ten).

Why output: it is what the model produced. Total tokens are dominated by cache reads, so a long conversation would level a model up without it doing more. Why fixed 1-2-5 targets: every next target is a round number you can read, and each level asks for 2 to 2.5 times the output of the one before, so levels keep meaning something at any scale.

Worked example: why “18.2M of 20M output” is progress to LV 15

A model has written 18.2M output tokens. The highest target reached is 10M, the 13th target, so it is LV 14. The next target is 20M, which makes LV 15. The bar on its card runs from 10M to 20M: (18.2M − 10M) ÷ (20M − 10M) = 82%, with 1.8M to go.

Rank and specialty

Rank: by level, then by output, then by total tokens. A higher rank never has a lower level.

Specialty ratio for a kind = (model's tokens of that kind ÷ model's total) ÷ (all models' tokens of that kind ÷ all models' total)

The specialty is the kind with the highest ratio, shown as, for example, “Reading input · 13× the party”: that model's share of input is 13 times the share across all models. If no kind reaches 1.1×, the model is an All-rounder. Every model mostly reads from cache, so the plain largest kind would say the same thing on every card; the ratio shows how each one differs. Source: ModelSpecialty.

Agents per task

Optional, off until you turn it on in Settings › Providers. A task is one prompt: from the moment you send it until Claude stops. Pulse adds four background hooks to Claude Code (prompt submitted, subagent started, subagent stopped, Claude stopped) and counts the subagents each task starts, nested ones included.

FigureMeaning
AgentsSubagents started during the task. Each one counts once, matched by a hashed id, even if its events arrive out of order.
TypesClaude Code's built-in types by name (Explore, Plan, General, Guide, Status line). Your own and plugin agents count as Custom; their names are never sent.
FinishedSubagents that reported stopping. Background subagents can finish after the task ends.
RunningNo stop seen yet, and the task started under six hours ago.
Used agentsShare of the last 30 days' tasks that started at least one subagent.
Agents per taskAll subagents in 30 days ÷ all tasks in 30 days, tasks without subagents included.

Claude Code hands every hook the whole event, your prompt included. The helper keeps only the event name, hashed session and agent ids and the type, and drops the rest before anything leaves it. Source: HookFrame, Store.record.

Limits and the pace estimate

StateWhen
AvailableUnder 80% used
Approaching limit80% or more
Nearly exhausted95% or more
Limit reached100%
Reset expectedThe reset time has passed but no fresh reading has confirmed it. Pulse never says “ready” without one.
StaleNo reading recently; the last value is shown as “last seen” with its age.

A state clears only after usage drops 3 points below its threshold, so it does not flicker. Source: EngineConfig in Sources/UsageEngine/PoolReducer.swift.

Pace estimate (always labelled “Estimate”): a straight line fitted by least squares through recent readings in the current window: the last 90 minutes for the 5-hour limit, the last 24 hours for the 7-day limit. It needs at least 3 readings over at least 10 minutes. Below 0.5 points per hour usage counts as flat (“not rising”). It never looks further ahead than 8 times the history it rests on, so an hour of readings cannot predict two days. If the window resets before the line reaches 100%, it says roughly how much will be used by the reset. Source: PaceEstimate in Sources/UsageEngine/Pace.swift.

The Limits chart spans the limit's own window, from reset minus its length to the reset. The hatched span before the first reading is time Pulse was not yet watching.

Activity calendar

Each square is a day. Shading is by messages that day, in quarters of your active days: the quietest quarter is the faintest green, the busiest quarter the brightest. A day with no messages is dark; a day before your first session or after today is an outline marked “no data”. All time shows one calendar year (January to December) with a year switcher; 30 days shows a month calendar; 7 days shows a column per day. Source: StatsCalendar and StatsSummary.

Charts

Tokens per day are stacked by model on a real date axis: days with nothing stay on the axis. Past 120 days the bars become weeks. The scale's top is the first round number at or above the tallest bar whose quarters are also round (for example 8M for a 6.5M peak). Models differ by colour and fill pattern, never colour alone. Point at a bar for its numbers.
